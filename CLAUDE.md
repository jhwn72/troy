# Troy

iMessage agent that connects USC students to the right person, office or item. Built Nov 14 to 15, 2026 for the AI@USC Hackathon. The full spec is `docs/troy-build-spec.md`; the why is `docs/troy-idea-brief.md`. When they disagree with this file, this file wins (it records later decisions).

## Run

- Node 24 runs TypeScript directly. No build step. Imports use `.ts` extensions. No enums, no parameter properties, no namespaces (type stripping only).
- `npm run dev` (server), `npm test` (node:test), `npm run typecheck`.
- SQLite through the built-in `node:sqlite` (`DatabaseSync`). Schema in `src/db/schema.sql`, helpers in `src/db/index.ts`.
- Web server is Hono on `@hono/node-server`. It must bind loopback only, on both `::1` and `127.0.0.1` (BlueBubbles resolves localhost to IPv6).
- `MESSAGING_PROVIDER=webchat` for development, `bluebubbles` for the real thing.
- Port 3000 is the BlueBubbles webhook target. While the old test listener holds it, develop on `PORT=3100`.

## Layout and ownership

- `src/types.ts`: shared contracts. Read it first. Don't change it casually.
- `src/adapters/`: one file per provider, plus `gate.ts` (pure privacy-gate functions) and `index.ts` (picks the provider).
- `src/pipeline.ts`: the message pipeline. `src/commands.ts`: code commands (STOP, HELP, emergency...). `src/onboarding.ts`.
- `src/ai.ts`: the only file that calls the Claude API.
- `src/flows/<name>.ts`: one `FlowHandler` per flow, registered in `src/flows/index.ts`. Lost and found lives in `src/flows/lostfound/` (`handler.ts` has the state table at the top, `match.ts`, `verify.ts`, `vision.ts`).
- `src/pending.ts`: one open question per person. A question for someone who is busy with another one waits in `pending_asks` and is asked when they are free. Use it instead of overwriting someone's `flow_state`.
- `src/outbox.ts`: dashboard texts held back by quiet hours or pause wait in `outbox` and go out later, in order. STOP drops them.
- `src/location.ts`: `saveUserLocation`, `forgetUserLocation`, `getUserLocation` (saved fix first, stale after 3 hours, then the first usual spot).
- `src/eval.ts` and `scripts/eval.ts`: the lost and found eval. `latestEval()` feeds the dashboard and the panel.
- `docs/flow-transcripts.md`: what Troy says today, captured from the running server.
- `public/`: static pages. `tests/`: node:test files. `scripts/`: one-off scripts.

## Privacy rules (never weaken these)

Troy runs on John's personal Apple ID, so BlueBubbles sees all of his iMessages.

1. Drop every event that is from John (`isFromMe`) before anything else.
2. A chat is allowed only if: its first message after go-live starts with "hey troy", or its handle is on the manual list (`ALLOWED_HANDLES` or the dashboard), or it's a group chat Troy created.
3. Everything else is ignored completely: no save, no read receipt, no typing, no tapback, no reply, never sent to Claude, never logged. At most log the literal string `ignored event`, with no handle and no text.
4. A chat with any message from before go-live can never be allowed by the "hey troy" rule. Look up only the date of its earliest message, never the text.
5. The adapter itself refuses to send, react, edit, mark read or type in a chat that isn't allowed. This check lives inside the adapter.
6. `TROY_ENABLED` (env and the `troy_enabled` setting) stops all sending and processing.
7. Never print or commit secrets. Never paste BlueBubbles' own log anywhere (it has previews from every chat).
8. When testing against the real BlueBubbles server, touch only the test chat (the handles in `ALLOWED_HANDLES`).

## Decisions (John, Nov 14 afternoon)

- **Edits are allowed again** (John, Nov 14 evening). The stock BlueBubbles 1.9.12 helper crashed Messages on edits; the patched helper in `bluebubbles/` fixes it. `editMessage` only calls the endpoint when `BLUEBUBBLES_EDITS=true`, and returns false otherwise or on failure. Callers must always have the fallback: Flow 2 edits the alert count when it can and sends a new short text ("last one", "all gone") when it can't.
- **`bluebubbles/` belongs to the BlueBubbles setup session.** Don't edit or commit it from here. Port 45699 is reserved for its helper channel.
- **No-AI verification leans on the finder** (John, Nov 14 evening). When `scoreDescription` comes back with source `fallback` (the Claude API was down or slow), Troy never passes a claimant on that score alone. Instead the finder decides: Troy shows the finder the owner's own description (never the other way around) and asks if it sounds like the item's owner; the finder's yes verifies (method `finder_confirm`), a no counts as a miss. The fallback score is only a hint and a safety brake: a clear contradiction (wrong name, wrong color or size) still counts as a miss without bothering the finder. Items already at DPS go to "dps can help u in person".
- **Trust USG's own statements** (John, Nov 14 evening). The USG president said at the opening session that USG runs an LAX shuttle, a farmers market and thrift, and funds RSOs. Troy states those programs exist plainly, without hedging. Dates, prices and links still only come from confirmed sources; when there isn't one, Troy says to ask USG for this year's dates.
- **Welcome copy and texting feel** (John, Nov 14 evening, after the first live test). Welcome bubbles, John's words: "hey i'm troy" / "i'm an ai, but i know a guy for everything" / "send me a photo of anything on campus and i'll get it to the right person. or just text me like 'lost my airpods at leavey'". STOP line: "reply STOP anytime and i'll leave u alone until u want to talk again". Before the contact card: "save my contact if u haven't so u know it's me"; the card carries the mascot photo. The judge skip is a poll ("usc student" / "hackathon judge"), not a typed JUDGE reply. "Read" must show before Troy starts typing; typing shows 1 to 2 seconds for normal bubbles and 3 to 4 for longer ones; long replies are split into smaller chunks.
- **Browser demo limits** (John, Nov 14 evening). The public browser demo allows 25 messages per visitor and 100 for judges. Judge status is honor system: the line above the judge poll or button asks non-judges kindly not to press it. At the limit Troy says it's a hackathon demo and offers to ask John for more. Each visitor can send exactly one request for more; it emails OWNER_EMAIL a link where John approves a specific amount. Emergency and crisis replies still go out after STOP, and self-harm messages get the fixed 988 reply (John confirmed both). The GitHub repo goes public right before submission, not earlier.
- **Hosting** (John, Nov 14 evening). Public pages go on Vercel, not Replit. The iMessage server stays on John's Mac. The landing page is static and deploys as is; a public browser demo on Vercel needs a hosted database because serverless functions don't keep files or memory between requests.
- **Troy's chat brain** (John, Nov 14 night). Outside locked steps Troy talks freely from the files in `brain/` (persona, about-troy, playbook), never from canned replies. Locked steps lock the facts, not the wording. `brain/origin-story.md` is background only: he never quotes or recites it, he answers in his own words.
- **Explaining how he works** (John, updated Nov 14 late). Two modes, set by `TROY_EXPLAIN`. `detailed` (the hackathon setting): he explains how he actually works when asked, in his own voice: built on Claude (a stronger model reads photos, matches and verifies; a faster one chats), texts through iMessage from a Mac running BlueBubbles, a small database, a staff dashboard, mocked links into office systems. `generic` (for a real campus launch): "my brain runs on ai", "i text over imessage", no vendors or wiring. In BOTH modes he never gives the verification answer key: how many tries, what passes, which details are hidden. He always actually answers; a reply that only says what he won't share is a failed reply.
- **Break-attempt plan** (`docs/break-troy-plan.md`). Apply its principles through the brief first and code checks second, and follow the critic: when in doubt he answers like a person, not a policy; do not add checks that reject normal friendly replies; do not reverse earlier decisions (finder's no is a miss; after STOP only emergency and crisis replies); no judge-account isolation. The demo path (two phones, photo find, typed loss, describe, handoff) must have an automated rehearsal test that runs with every check on.
- **Jokes versus real distress** (John). The model tells them apart, not word patterns. Only unmistakable emergencies stay on fixed patterns. Anything ambiguous ("that exam killed me lol", "kms") gets a quick read of the message in its conversation: a joke gets a normal reply; real worry gets Troy checking in like a person, one direct kind question, no hotline wall; a yes or a dodge after that gets the fixed crisis line. Wrong toward caring is fine; a hotline on obvious slang is a failure.
- **About John and USC** (John). Troy never swears. He may use John's full name when telling the origin story. Cost: free for students. USC: it hasn't been implemented with USC yet, John is hoping it will be; nobody at USC has been told because it's a hackathon project. For anything deeper: ask John. "delete my data" also removes photos and listings. Live animals are out of scope like dangerous items. (Superseded below: the alert poll keeps all four options and gains "something else".)
- **Quick lookup for things he doesn't know** (John). For a factual campus question the directory can't answer, Troy may do one cheap, fast web lookup and give a best guess with "double check". Keep compute low: one search at most, the fast model, official USC pages preferred, a per-student daily cap, and never for anything a hard rule covers.
- **Location ask** (John, via the BlueBubbles session). Default is a text plus the how-to GIF at `bluebubbles/gif/share-location.gif`: "btw, i work best when i know where u are. here's how to share ur location with me (takes 10 seconds, turn it off anytime) 👇". Then one bubble saying he keeps the last spot they share and that "forget my location" erases it. The Find My request is a fallback only. Troy always writes "u" and "ur".

If real open ride requests from other users match, he uses those and doesn't add the note. He still mentions the USG LAX shuttle.

With nobody assigned, the dashboard shows the text Troy would have sent, labeled as an example.
  - **Technician.** Same mechanism for FMS and Housing: an assigned handle gets "someone reported ... at [building], about an N minute walk from u. can u take it?", and their "on it" and "done" (with an optional photo) move the status and text the student. With nobody assigned, the report still goes to the dashboard, which shows the example text to a technician and keeps the status buttons.
  - The finder always chooses the handoff (meet or DPS), on the page and in the product.
- **Onboarding copy, round two** (John, after testing on his phone, Nov 14 night). After the welcome: "save my contact if u haven't so u know it's me", the card, then a slightly longer beat (about 2 seconds) before the next bubble. Then: "quick check, are u a usc student or a hackathon judge? (pls be honest)" followed by a poll with two options, "usc student" and "hackathon judge". Student: "great, what's ur usc email?" then the normal code flow. Judge: "thanks for checking me out. i'll go ahead and skip verification for u". The old lines ("quick thing, gotta make sure ur a trojan...", "hackathon judges can skip this...") are gone. Typed answers still work.
- **Quotes around exact words to type** (John). Whenever Troy tells someone the exact thing to text, it goes in double quotes: text "hey troy" if u ever want back in; text "forget my location" anytime; reply "STOP". When he's only describing what to do in general ("lmk if u ever want back in"), no quotes. Applies to fixed lines, variant pools and the free chat brief.
- **Onboarding has discretion too** (John, Nov 14 night: he texted "can we start over" at the email step and got "that doesn't look like an email"). Each onboarding step knows what it is waiting for (a poll choice, an email, a code, a location answer). When a message is that thing, code handles it exactly as now. When it isn't, Troy does not repeat a format error: the chat brain answers like a person, knowing which step is open, and can take the real actions that fit: start over, go back to the student-or-judge question, resend the code, change the email, explain why he asks, skip an optional step. Code still does every check (the @usc.edu rule, the code match, attempt limits). A plain format hint ("it ends in @usc.edu") is only for something that looks like an attempted email. A failed email send says so honestly and offers a way forward instead of asking for the same email again.
- **Commands by meaning** (John, Nov 14 night). Students aren't bound to exact command words. The exact words (STOP, START, HELP, "delete my data", "forget my location", pause, resume) still act instantly in code. Anything that means the same ("stop talking to me", "leave me alone", "unsubscribe", "delete my account", "erase everything u have on me", "stop the food texts") is read in context by the model and mapped to the same code action. Rules: a clear wish to be left alone is honored at once with the normal STOP confirmation; anything that destroys data asks once first ("want me to delete everything i have on u? reply \"yes\""); a partial wish ("stop the food texts") changes that setting, not everything; playful or mid-sentence uses ("stop lol", "stop ur so funny", "i can't stop laughing") do nothing; when Troy can't tell, he asks one short question instead of guessing. Code performs every action and sends the fixed confirmation; the model only decides which action was meant.
- **Forgiving onboarding and thumbs-up as yes** (John, Nov 14 night). Small slips don't bounce the student. Spaces, a trailing period, wrong case and similar noise in an email are fixed silently. A likely typo ("@usc,edu", "@usc.eud", "@uscedu", "@usc.com", a space in the middle) gets "did u mean \"name@usc.edu\"? if so i'll send the code now", and yes, yep, any variation, or a 👍 confirms. Same spirit for codes (spaces or dashes in the six digits are fine).
  A 👍 (or ❤️) tapback counts as yes when it lands on Troy's most recent question that is still open. Edge cases where it does not: a tapback on any bubble that isn't the open question is just an acknowledgment; a question that has expired or been answered ignores it; deleting data needs a typed yes; verification steps (describing an item, a code, a name) can't be answered with a tapback; a 👎 on the open question counts as no; on a food alert a 👍 keeps meaning "claim one". The contact-sharing warning accepts a 👍 only on that exact warning bubble.
- **Open-ended alert topics** (John, Nov 14 night). The "what do u want texts about?" poll keeps free food, free stuff, events, help others with classes, and adds "something else". Picking it (or typing a topic at any time) starts a short, free conversation, not a canned branch: Troy asks what they have in mind, then code checks whether a matching topic already exists (the four built in, plus topics other students created; match loosely, e.g. "study buddies" and "study groups"). If it exists he adds them. If it doesn't and it's reasonable (meeting new friends, language tutors, pickup basketball, anything niche but fine for a campus), he creates it, adds them, and makes them feel good about it in his own words: it didn't exist, it's a great idea, and it's now an option for everyone. Created topics are stored (who suggested it, when) and offered to later students; staff can remove one from the dashboard's Moderation tab. Inappropriate or unsafe topics (drugs, anything illegal, harassment, things aimed at a person) are declined in one easy line, never created. **Topics are real** (John): any verified student can post to a topic they follow ("anyone want to play pickup at lyon at 6", "spanish tutor free thursdays"), and Troy alerts the topic's subscribers who are near enough and not in quiet hours, reusing the food alert engine (location, alert distance, rate limits, report and ban). Three outcomes when a student suggests a topic, each in Troy's own varied words, never one fixed line: clearly fine, so create it and celebrate it; unclear or hard to judge right now, so no topic is created, the request is saved for John to review on the dashboard, and Troy says he'll text them when something comes up for it; highly inappropriate, so a short "sorry, can't help with that" style decline.
- **Alert poll follow-through** (John, tested on his phone Nov 14 night). iMessage polls do take several votes. After the first votes Troy confirms what he got in his own words and adds only that they can add more later; he does not repeat the instructions ("text the numbers like 1 and 3, or text done"). The poll stays live: a vote added after he already confirmed is saved and acknowledged briefly ("got that one too", "got those too", varied), and a vote removed later is dropped the same way. Several votes arriving close together get one acknowledgment, not one each. This works at any time after onboarding, not only during it.
- **Alert poll options and the "what else" line** (John, Nov 14 night). The alerts poll options, in order: "free food and stuff nearby 🌯", "rides home and to lax ✈️", "extra tickets for game day 🏈", "pickup games right now 🏀", "experienced trojans 💼", "something else". These replace the earlier four. Each is a real topic (see "Topics are real"); "experienced trojans" means getting connected to older students who've done the thing you're asking about (classes, recruiting, clubs). After the student picks, Troy confirms and then tells them once what else he's for, in his own varied words, covering these four things: finding something someone lost, losing something themselves, spotting something broken on campus, and having a complaint they want student government to see; they just tell him and he connects them. Cleaned-up reference wording (not a script): "got it. also, if u ever find something someone lost, lose something urself, spot something broken on campus, or have a complaint u want student government to see, just tell me and i'll connect u".
- **The first ten minutes** (John, Nov 14 night). After onboarding Troy carries six moves and plays them one at a time; he never shows them as a menu.
  1. Always, right when onboarding ends: the "what else" line, then the "near u" bubbles. Free food only if they picked that topic: "btw there's free pizza about 8 min from u. want the spot?". Then: "also 3 people reported losing something near u. lmk if u want details so u can keep an eye out". Use real nearby drops and lost reports when they exist (counts only, never item details). When there are none, use example numbers without labeling them; the moment the student asks for the spot or the details, Troy says plainly that this is a hackathon build and those were examples, describes what it looks like when it's real, and offers the try-it tour. Nobody is ever sent to walk to something that isn't there.
  2. Try-it tour: the person plays the owner against a labeled example found item and goes through the real blind check and handoff (also an example food claim, and "send any photo" for repairs). Offered when they show curiosity; for judges it is Troy's first move after the skip.
  3. Stump me ("name any campus problem and i'll tell u who handles it"): only when the chat is casual or they ask what he can do.
  4. The one question ("what's one thing u wish was easier at usc?"): only if they've gone quiet for a couple of minutes without starting anything; the answer goes somewhere real (a USG concern, a topic, a note).
  5. Set up something for later ("where do u usually study?"): asked the first time a walking time would be missing, not up front.
  6. One follow-up on his own, six to eight minutes in, tied to what they did or said.
  Rules: at most one unprompted message in the first hour (if two qualify he picks the better fit); a real request clears all of it; never the same move twice with the same person; pause and STOP end it; every line is in his own varied words.
- **First ten minutes, additions** (John, right after). Instructions say WHAT he must get across; HOW he types it is his: wording, rhythm, how many bubbles, whether to joke. Right after the "near u" bubbles he sends the "try me" poll immediately (not after 30 seconds of silence). The source list is `docs/design-decisions.md`; the same list feeds the landing page, the write-up, the slides and the video so judges see the same reasoning everywhere.
- **Voice messages**: wanted later, after the MVP flows run on real phones. Plan: audio attachments transcribed on the Mac with the built-in speech recognition, then treated as text.
- **Chat model.** Free chat runs on the stronger model (`TROY_CHAT_MODEL=smart`): a live test passed 13 of 13 there against 45 of 52 on the fast one, for about 0.4 s more per reply.
- **Judge skip.** At the USC email step Troy offers a clearly labeled skip for hackathon judges. It is a poll ("usc student" / "hackathon judge"), not a typed reply, with a visible button in web forms. Sets `verifiedVia = 'judge_skip'`. Copy must say it's for this demo only and wouldn't exist in the real campus version.
- **Polls work natively** through the patched helper when it is connected (`sendPoll` returns mode `native`, votes arrive as `pollVote`). The fallback is tap to pick (one bubble per option, a heart or thumbs up picks it), then a numbered list. Callers handle all three modes.
- **Adapter additions.** `requestLocation(chatId)` sends the Find My request bubble when it can and returns false when it can't (then Troy just asks). Optional `onAllowed(chatId)` and `onDisallowed(chatId)` are called by the pipeline right after opt-in and on STOP or "delete my data".
- **Lost and found: what the owner is told never depends on the match score.** Every claim opens with the same bubbles and the same blind question. The old "80+ verifies, 50 to 79 gets one more question" wording is gone. The AI score only orders candidates and shows on the dashboard; whether a pair is acted on is decided by a public score (kind, color, brand, place, time) of 50 or more.
- **Every scored description is a counted try.** Tries are counted per found item and person, two at most. Backing out, "nvm" or a new report resets nothing.
- **Silent claimants are released.** After 1 hour when someone else is waiting on the item, after 24 hours otherwise. STOP and "delete my data" release at once, and every release looks for the next owner.
- **Lost and found texts to the other person ignore quiet hours** (`sayToOwed`): the question for the finder, the match for the owner, the contact warning and the pickup code are owed, and a dropped one would leave a step open on words nobody saw. Nothing is ever sent after STOP.
- **Pending questions and the outbox.** One open step per person. A question for someone mid-question is parked and asked when they are free (`src/pending.ts`). Dashboard texts held by quiet hours or pause wait in the outbox (`src/outbox.ts`).
- **Eval in fallback mode** reports a rule-score pass as "sent to the finder", never as a handoff. `EvalResult` has `sentToFinder` and `sentToFinderNotOwner` next to the older fields.
- **Location is kept** (John, Nov 14 evening; see "Location" in the spec). A share in the chat gives one fix, read through the patched helper; there are no continuous updates. Troy saves each user's last known location (lastLat, lastLng, lastLocationAt, lastLocationSource) and uses it for walking times, refreshing it on every new share, pin or "where are u rn?" answer, with usual spots as the fallback. Never show a saved location to another student or on a dashboard. STOP, "delete my data" and "forget my location" erase it.
- **Emergency words** match phrases ("there's a fire", "i'm hurt", "being followed"), not bare words. "that's fire" must not trigger it.
- **Redaction** on dashboards is a full-image blur for wallets, IDs and cards, with click to reveal.
- **Cut rules** in the spec are reminders, not hard cutoffs.

## Hard rules (code decides, never the model)

The gate, send lock and kill switch; welcome and STOP text; emergency replies; pickup codes; servings counts and claims; walking times; verification results and attempt limits; dangerous-item routing; what claimants see before verification; contact rules; posting bans; any office, committee or program name, contact detail, date or link.
